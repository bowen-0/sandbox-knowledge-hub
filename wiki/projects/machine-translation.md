# Machine Translation

Recommendations for public-administration adoption of machine translation for official documents [(Machine Translation report, p. 2)](../sources/p1-machine-translation.md) [(Machine Translation report, p. 19)](../sources/p1-machine-translation.md). Examines the **confidentiality and security requirements** that apply when administrative text flows through a third-party MT service: translated information typically leaves the administration's servers, so documents classified "confidential" or "secret" may only be machine-translated via internal or certified external solutions (e.g. ISO 27001), with reputation, liability and transparency duties on top [(Machine Translation report, p. 8)](../sources/p1-machine-translation.md).

## Outcome

The downstream **commercial-register-extracts MT solution** was, after a successful test with **Kanton Schwyz** (the Phase I case partner), rolled out to several cantons; [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §01 cites it as a Phase I roll-out success alongside [[smart-parking]]. The booklet records: *"Following successful testing with the Canton of Schwyz, the machine translation solution for commercial register extracts has been rolled out to several cantons."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 5)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

## Key learnings

- Established that **third-party machine translation** of confidential or secret administrative documents comes into question only under strict conditions. The report states: *"Machine translation of documents that include confidential or secret information is only conceivable if the following conditions are met: The solution is installed on the public administration’s internal servers or the external solution concerned is certified (e.g. according to ISO 27001). Full transparency regarding data flows and cybersecurity is guaranteed. The public authority concerned audits and authorises the solution."* [(Machine Translation report, p. 8)](../sources/p1-machine-translation.md)
- Linked in the corpus to the FADP Art. 21 / Constitution Art. 29 framing for automated administrative decisions ([[fadp-art-21]], [[bv-art-29]]); that framing is articulated in the [[building-permits]] legal analysis [(Building Permits report, p. 25)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) [(Building Permits report, p. 26)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md).
- Demonstrates the cross-cantonal scaling pathway for sandbox outputs: tested with Kanton Schwyz, then rolled out to several cantons [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 5)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
