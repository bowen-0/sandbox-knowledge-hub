# Frontier Models

> *"Highly advanced, large-scale AI models (e.g. large language or multimodal models) representing the current state of the art. They are trained on extensive datasets using substantial computational resources and underpin many generative AI applications, while also introducing heightened requirements for safety, governance, and regulation."*
> — [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]], glossary [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 26)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

The Build & Share booklet (§04 Technology, "Combine frontier models and open-source solutions strategically") makes the *combination* of frontier models + open-source models a strategic recommendation rather than a binary choice — requirements vary across data-protection, performance, cost, and scalability [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 17)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

The single most rigorous test of frontier-model behaviour in the corpus is [[building-permits]]: 8 frontier models benchmarked [(Building Permits report, p. 10)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) across 3,336 evaluations [(Building Permits report, p. 11)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) using [[llm-as-a-judge]]. Headline finding: **no single model wins** [(Building Permits report, p. 14)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) — model-agnostic architecture is mandatory [(Building Permits report, p. 13)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md).
