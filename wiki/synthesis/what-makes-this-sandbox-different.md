# What makes the Zurich AI Sandbox different — and what it deliberately is not

> **For:** a public-sector team comparing the Zurich model against an accelerator or a classic regulatory sandbox before designing their own, and a policymaker who needs the one-paragraph answer to "what is this, exactly?"

The label "sandbox" is doing a lot of work. A reader who hears it thinks of one of two familiar instruments: a start-up accelerator (funding, mentoring, scaling a company) or a regulatory sandbox (a supervisor granting controlled exemptions so a firm can test outside the normal rules). The Zurich AI Innovation Sandbox is neither, and its own reports are explicit about the design choices that separate it from both. The defining move is a combination — real-world testing, regulatory clarification, public-sector collaboration, data access, and knowledge transfer — held together in a single programme, with each project a selected use case rather than an open-ended engagement with one company.

This page describes what the *programme* is, for anyone considering running one like it. It is not deployment advice.

## Not mainly a funding or scaling programme

An accelerator's core offer is capital and growth support for a company. The Zurich model offers neither. Participation carries no financial compensation, and participating organisations contribute their own personnel and bring their own AI technology — the first of the five application requirements is "Own AI competencies": *"The participating organisations must provide their own AI technologies and possess the necessary technological expertise."* [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 12)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md). The no-compensation rule is a deliberate design choice retained from Phase I into Phase II, alongside open cross-sector calls and the within-existing-law constraint [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 5)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

The programme also does not take responsibility for scaling the resulting solution into production. Continuation after the test phase sits with the project partners, not the sandbox: *"Continued implementation is not an explicit objective of the sandbox; it remains the responsibility of the respective project partners."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 5)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). When a pilot does scale — Smart Parking went into production in Frauenfeld, the commercial-register machine translation rolled out to several cantons — the booklet reads that as evidence of practical relevance, not as a service the programme delivered [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 5)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## Not only a regulatory-interpretation desk

A classic regulatory sandbox is a supervisor's instrument: it clears legal questions, and sometimes suspends a rule for a controlled trial. The Zurich programme does the regulatory-clarification work, but its design explicitly rejects two things a regulatory desk would do.

First, it tests in the real world rather than ruling on paper. Phase I frames this as the differentiator against other countries' approaches: *"In contrast to the sandbox approaches of other countries, which primarily carry out legal assessments, this model incorporates practical testing and the use of AI technologies in the real world."* [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 10)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md). The Phase I design conclusion lays this out as an explicit fork — "Legal assessment (theoretical evaluation without testing)" versus "Practical implementation (real-world testing with partners)" — and records that the steering committee chose practical implementation [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 26)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md). Phase II restates the same priority at the top: the focus is on real-world testing rather than purely regulatory reviews [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 4)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

Second, it does not grant exemptions — the booklet states it flatly: *"The sandbox operates deliberately within existing legal frameworks and does not allow for the suspension of regulatory requirements."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Phase I's design page makes the trade-off conscious: instruments such as experimentation clauses and no-action letters are valuable but are not available across the relevant Swiss legal domains, so the programme chose the *"Current legal framework conditions (no specific instruments)"* path [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 26)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md). What the sandbox produces is structured regulatory clarity, not a binding decision: Phase I notes that *"the sandbox results do not constitute definitive decisions by a competent authority"* [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 27)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md).

So the regulatory work is real, but it is one strand of a wider instrument, and it clarifies rather than exempts.

## What it combines instead

The distinctiveness is the bundle, not any single element. Across both phases the programme holds five things together that the two familiar instruments keep apart:

- **Real-world testing.** Three of the five Phase II projects combined regulatory questions with concrete real-world testing of the AI system in the public sector, yielding findings on operation, integration and governance that paper analysis would not surface [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 9)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Real-world testing is one of the booklet's named terms of art (see *real-world testing*, [[real-world-testing]]).
- **Regulatory clarification.** Each project produces a structured reading of the legal requirements, reducing uncertainty for both the provider and the supervisor [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 20)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
- **Public-sector collaboration and data access.** The programme brokers access to test partners, real application contexts and public-sector datasets — the access that an AI provider cannot easily obtain alone, and the lever the corpus rates highest (see *data access*, [[data-access]]) [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 20)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Which partners are needed, and in which of five roles, is set out in [[partner-roles-around-a-pilot]].
- **Knowledge transfer.** Results are systematically prepared, published transparently, and made available for organisations facing similar questions [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 4)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Willingness to publish is a condition of entry, not an afterthought: applicants must agree to publish their findings and contribute to best practices, IP excepted [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 12)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md).

Von Thiessen's framing names the through-line: *"The AI sandbox systematically translates concrete projects into broader impact: from practical application and knowledge transfer to the shaping of AI policy."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 20)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## Selected use cases, not company coaching

An accelerator works with a company; the sandbox works with a use case. Projects are selected through a portfolio process — a low-threshold written submission, a 30-minute validation interview, and prioritisation by the steering committee — and run as a managed portfolio of distinct use cases rather than as bespoke support for any one organisation's business [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 6)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The intake screens for clearly bounded use cases over open, problem-oriented pitches; in the health sector specifically, precisely defined AI services and demonstrated domain experience were decisive, while generic health chatbots or providers without a concrete use case proved less suitable [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 7)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The unit of work is the use case, and the portfolio is steered with rolling budgeting toward where it yields the most value [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 8)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## Outputs are meant to benefit the wider ecosystem

The reach the programme aims for is the clearest break from a company-centric accelerator. The booklet structures impact as three building levels: direct value for the participating AI providers and public partners; know-how transfer into the wider ecosystem through published reports, selected open prototypes, regulatory roundtables and international networking; and systemic anchoring, where project findings feed political and regulatory processes [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 20)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The second and third levels exist precisely so a finding does not stay with the single organisation that produced it: non-competitive results such as methodological approaches, regulatory classifications and reference architectures are deliberately made accessible [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 9)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

The systemic level is documented, not asserted: the building-permits work fed a cantonal-parliament postulate on AI in the permit procedure; the sandbox model fed the Federal Council's decision on AI regulation, which anchored sandboxes as an innovation-promotion instrument; and practical experience was passed to sector initiatives in education (Educa), health (Swissmedic) and mobility (MODI) [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 21)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The programme also connects the actors a single accelerator would not: government, academia, companies and implementation partners sit around each pilot through the five partner roles [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 8)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md), and the model now sits inside a four-pillar cantonal ecosystem strategy through 2029 ([[zh-ai-ecosystem-strategy-2026-2029]]) [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 24)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

> [!tension] One claim is corpus synthesis, not a single statement
> No single sentence in the reports says "we combine these five things, and that is what makes us different." The five-element framing here is a synthesis across both overview reports — the no-compensation and within-existing-law design choices from Phase I, the three impact levels and the real-world-testing priority from Phase II. The *individual* claims are direct statements, each page-anchored above; the *bundle as the differentiator* is the wiki's reading of them. Treat it as portfolio-level synthesis, one tier below a direct quotation in the evidence hierarchy.

## Limits of the model

The same reports that set out the strengths name the limitations directly, in the dedicated impact-and-scaling chapter [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). They belong in any honest answer to "what is this?"

It is high-touch and does not scale easily. Running a sandbox is resource-intensive; the booklet states plainly that scaling from five to, say, fifty projects would demand substantial personnel and organisational investment, and that *"natural limits to scalability remain"* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The roughly 18-month call cadence compounds this: between calls, no new project can enter, and given the speed of AI development a missed window means delayed findings [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Why it cannot scale cheaply is treated in detail in the answer to "what prevented the sandbox from scaling more rapidly" (this synthesis does not yet have its own page; see the validation question on scaling constraints).

The portfolio is small, and the bottom-up design narrows what it can see. Five implemented projects per phase is, by construction, a thin slice of the possible use-case space; the booklet notes that this deliberate focus on a few clearly defined use cases keeps the findings practical but bounded, and raises the stakes when an individual project does not run as planned [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Working strictly inside existing law further bounds the knowledge space: it limits insight into potentially innovative but not yet legally permissible approaches — *"Certain questions therefore cannot be fully explored and remain constrained by the current legal framework"* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 22)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Whole sectors are absent from the portfolio as a result: only one of 45 Phase I submissions came from the financial sector [(Play & Learn (Phase I overview report), p. 11)](../sources/00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn.md), and the broader set of underrepresented domains is treated in the answer to "where is there still need for further clarification" (also not yet a standalone page).

Phase II's own conclusion treats these as a roadmap rather than a verdict: more agile, more frequent intake, a tiered "funnel" from self-service through a regulatory desk to full sandbox support, and thematic portfolio calls are all proposed precisely to ease the scaling and cadence limits above [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 23)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

> [!update-needed] Reflects Phase II practice (2024–2026)
> This describes the sandbox as it operated through Phase II. The Conclusion and Outlook chapter already proposes changes — agile intake, a tiered support funnel, thematic calls, and political clarification of whether testing could one day run under defined conditions outside the existing legal frame [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 23)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). Several of these would alter the "no exemptions" and "doesn't scale" characterisations above. Re-check against the Phase III state of practice when it lands.
