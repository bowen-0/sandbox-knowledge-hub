# EMBAG — Federal Act on the Use of Electronic Means

The Swiss Federal Act on the Use of Electronic Means for Government Tasks (*Bundesgesetz über den Einsatz elektronischer Mittel zur Erfüllung von Behördenaufgaben*, EMBAG). SR 172.019, dated 17 March 2023, regulating **at federal level** since 2024 [(Building Permits report, p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md). Regulates Swiss e-government infrastructure — most notably **the disclosure of administrative source code** (open-source by default) and the publication of administrative data. The building-permits legal chapter summarises its reach: *"At the federal level, EMBAG has regulated since 2024 the use of electronic means to fulfil governmental tasks, such as the transfer of electronic administrative assistance activities to private individuals, the disclosure of source codes and the publication of procured and generated administrative data – which cantons and municipalities are increasingly oriented towards."* [(Building Permits report, p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md).

## Why it matters for the corpus

[[building-permits]] explicitly engages EMBAG [(Building Permits report, p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) because the prototype is being developed with public funds for use in administrative procedures. EMBAG binds the **federal** administration — including the default disclosure of administrative source code as open-source — and the report notes that they are not directly bound but that EMBAG is a norm *"which cantons and municipalities are increasingly oriented towards"* (p. 24). That orientation effect, rather than a direct legal obligation, is what shapes how the prototype's deployment to *eBaugesucheZH* (the cantonal building-permit platform) is structured.

The pattern recurs: [[digital-eye-clinic]] also went the open-source route — though that project's primary driver was the absence of fit-for-purpose commercial alternatives ([[intrapreneurship]]).
