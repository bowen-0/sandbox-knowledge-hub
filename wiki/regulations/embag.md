# EMBAG — Federal Act on the Use of Electronic Means

The Swiss Federal Act on the Use of Electronic Means for Government Tasks (*Bundesgesetz über den Einsatz elektronischer Mittel zur Erfüllung von Behördenaufgaben*, EMBAG). SR 172.019, in force 17 March 2023 [(p2-building-permits p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md). Regulates Swiss e-government infrastructure — most notably **the disclosure of administrative source code** (open-source by default) and the publication of administrative data. The building-permits legal chapter summarises its reach: *«Auf Bundesebene regelt das EMBAG seit 2024 den Einsatz elektronischer Mittel zur Erfüllung von Behördenaufgaben, wie die Übertragung administrativer elektronischer Hilfstätigkeit auf Private, die Offenlegung von Quellcodes und die Veröffentlichung von beschafften und generierten Verwaltungsdaten – woran sich auch zunehmend Kantone und Gemeinden orientieren.»* (since 2024 the EMBAG has regulated, at federal level, the use of electronic means for government tasks, including the delegation of administrative electronic support activities to private parties, the disclosure of source code and the publication of procured and generated administrative data, with cantons and municipalities increasingly orienting themselves towards it) [(p2-building-permits p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md).

## Why it matters for the corpus

[[building-permits]] explicitly engages EMBAG [(p2-building-permits p. 24)](../sources/p2-building-permits.md) because the prototype is being developed with public funds for use in administrative procedures. Under EMBAG, the source code of public-administration software is generally required to be published as open-source — affecting how the prototype's deployment to *eBaugesucheZH* (the cantonal building-permit platform) is structured.

The implication generalises: any sandbox prototype that subsequently becomes part of public administration is presumptively subject to EMBAG's open-source disclosure requirement. This is one of the reasons [[digital-eye-clinic]] also went the open-source route — though that project's primary driver was the absence of fit-for-purpose commercial alternatives ([[intrapreneurship]]).
