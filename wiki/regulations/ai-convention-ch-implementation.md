# Swiss Implementation of the Council of Europe AI Convention

Switzerland's implementation of the Council of Europe's *Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law* — the first international legally-binding instrument specifically on AI, opened for signature 2024. The Swiss Federal Council's implementation decision came in early 2025; this is referenced in the booklet as the Federal Council's decision on AI regulation (*Bundesratsentscheid zur KI-Regulierung*) [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 21)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## Relevance to the corpus

The booklet's §04 legal findings in [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] state:

> *"AI does not operate in a legal vacuum: existing legal frameworks, e.g. in data protection and sector-specific regulation, are central in all projects and already address many of the key issues. AI-specific regulation, such as the implementation of the AI Convention into Swiss law, complements this framework in a targeted manner, especially in areas where AI systems affect fundamental rights."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 18)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

This positions the Convention as the **fundamental-rights backstop** to sector-specific regulation rather than as a replacement for it. The framing matters for any system that processes data in ways that affect individual rights — which is the entire Phase II health-sector portfolio.

## Sandbox impact

The booklet ([[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §05) credits the sandbox with concrete input into the *Bundesratsentscheid zur KI-Regulierung*: sandbox findings on testing environments, cross-border collaboration, and sector-specific compliance fed into the federal-level decision [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 21)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md). The report records, under the bullet "Policy impulses at the national level":

> *"Contributions to the further development of AI policy instruments, particularly in the context of the Federal Council’s decision on AI regulation, which established sandboxes as an instrument for promoting innovation, as well as providing a basis for a parliamentary initiative from the Council of States on AI testing environments."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 21)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)
