# Digital Gatekeepers: Exploring Large Language Model's Role in Immigration Decisions

An external academic study (Mao & Zhao, Maastricht University and University of Antwerp, June 2025) testing whether GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 can support immigration admission decisions, and whether they do so fairly. It is not a Sandbox pilot; it sits in the Knowledge Repository as outside-Switzerland corroboration of the finding that generative AI belongs out of the binding administrative decision step.

## Why it sits alongside the corpus

The [[building-permits]] project raised the question from the reliability side: can a generative model carry a legally relevant administrative decision? Immigration admission is exactly that kind of decision, and this study measures the fairness side of the same question. Its findings bear on [[rule-based-beats-generative-for-defined-logic]] and [[human-oversight-in-ai-deployment]].

## Method (for weighting the evidence)

10,000 discrete-choice immigration scenarios put to GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 in the role of a US immigration officer, plus structured interviews, analysed with a multinomial-logit utility model. English-language, US framing; a preprint, not peer-reviewed, so weight it as corroborating rather than primary evidence.
