# Build & Share — Phase II overview

**Read the report:** [PDF (DE)](../pdfs/de/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.pdf) · [PDF (EN)](../pdfs/en/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.pdf)

The 28-page final report of Phase II (2024–2026), published May 2026. The single most strategic document in the corpus. Where [[00-overview-phase1-play-and-learn]] explained why the sandbox exists, this booklet explains **where it is now** and **where it is going**: the four-pillar 2026–2029 ZH ecosystem strategy (Sandbox · AI Start-up Support · SME Adoption · Public Dialogue) anchored by a Government Council decision. Contains three load-bearing taxonomies for the wiki: the **7-sector submission classification** (figure 1 p. 6), the **5 partner-role types** (p. 8 diagram), and an **11-term canonical glossary** (p. 26).

## Methodology

How the booklet's findings were produced:

- **Portfolio approach.** 8 use cases initiated with participating organisations; 5 implemented in parallel, lightly staggered, within an 18-month window, most completed within 9 to 12 months, steered by rolling budgeting (p. 8). Three of the eight were discontinued by mutual agreement (p. 15).
- **Selection.** 24 project applications, against 21 in the first call. The process was unchanged from Phase I: written submission via a low-threshold online form, a 30-minute validation interview, then evaluation and prioritisation by the Steering Committee (p. 6).
- **Retained Phase I success factors.** Open project calls across all sectors; clear focus on regulatory clarification and access to test partners and data sources; no financial compensation for participating organisations; all projects within existing law, with no regulatory exemptions; strong public communication for knowledge transfer (p. 5).
- **New in Phase II.** Targeted pre-submission contact with interested parties to clarify open questions and test relevance early (p. 6); closer networking of participants via events and workshops, entry into new sectors (especially healthcare), and targeted engagement with EU regulatory questions for the market access of local AI providers (p. 5).
- **Partner model.** Five role types around each project: test partners, mandated experts, technical implementation partners, domain sparring partners, supervisory authorities (p. 8).
- **Evidence base.** Real-world testing in three of the five projects ([[digital-eye-clinic]], [[building-permits]], [[bridge-monitoring]]); the other two were limited to regulatory analysis: [[medical-documentation]] because data-protection requirements ruled out operational use of patient data in a public hospital, [[inspection-robots]] because no suitable test partner was found (p. 9).

## Notable quotes

- *«Unser Sandbox-Ansatz nutzt gezielt die Stärken des Schweizer KI-Ökosystems: Kollaboration, Offenheit und klarer Praxisfokus.»* In English: "Our sandbox approach deliberately uses the strengths of the Swiss AI ecosystem: collaboration, openness and a clear practical focus." (Raphael von Thiessen, Programmleiter KI-Standort, Kanton Zürich, p. 4)
- *«Gerade im Gesundheitswesen braucht es geschützte Testräume, um regulatorische Anforderungen und Fortschritt effektiv zusammenzubringen.»* In English: "Precisely in healthcare, you need protected test spaces to effectively bring regulatory requirements and progress together." (Stefan Lienhard, CEO, digital health center bülach, p. 7)
