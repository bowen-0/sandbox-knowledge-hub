# Artificial Intelligence Adoption in Public Organizations: A Case Study

**Read the report:** [DOI: 10.24023/FutureJournal/2175-5825/2024.v16i1.860](https://doi.org/10.24023/FutureJournal/2175-5825/2024.v16i1.860)

> [!update-needed]
> **This source sits outside the Zurich AI Innovation Sandbox corpus.** It is a peer-reviewed academic paper (not a sandbox pilot report), covering a different jurisdiction (Brazil), a different institution (the Brazilian Federal Revenue Service, RFB), and a different research method (scoping literature review + 10 semi-structured interviews with public- and private-sector executives + a single case study). ## Provenance

Published 2024 in the *Future Studies Research Journal* (FIA Business School / FEA-USP, São Paulo), a double-blind-reviewed guest paper received 06/03/2024 [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 1)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md). ## Methodology

The study combines a multi-stage literature analysis, ten semi-structured interviews conducted August 2022–January 2023 with executives from public and private organizations, and a case study of the Brazilian Federal Revenue Service (RFB) [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 1)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md). A scoping review of 67 EBSCO-indexed articles (2013–2023) was narrowed to 44 after filtering out purely methodological or technical pieces, then coded into ten topical categories, of which "Ethics, fairness and justice" (16 citations) and "Data governance and management" (13) were the most frequent, while only 5 studies (~7%) were labeled "AI adoption" outright [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 8)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md). Interviewees spanned education, telecommunications, manufacturing, ICT, traffic management, buildings and grounds, urban planning, urban mobility, software, and healthcare, mixing direct and indirect public administration with private-sector roles [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 10)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md). Transcripts went through two rounds of manual coding (160 codes, 38 AI-adoption-relevant) and a Braun-and-Clarke six-step thematic analysis, converging on 63 focused codes and ten AI-adoption driver themes [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 11)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).

The case study examined SISAM (Sistema de Seleção Aduaneira por Aprendizado de Máquina / Customs Selection System Through Machine Learning), an RFB production system that processes all Import Declarations in Brazil in real time — over 2.5 million in 2022 — using a Bayesian network trained on more than 100 million SISCOMEX database entries and holding roughly 8.5 billion data points [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 14)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).

## Key findings

- **Ten AI-adoption drivers**, ranked by coded frequency: efficiency (21), workload (19), implementation and operations (18), privacy (18), quality (17), leadership (15), transparency (13), fairness and justice (9), trust (7), responsibility (6) [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 14)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Efficiency was the primary driver**, but transparency, technological readiness, skilled labor force, fairness, and privacy also substantially shaped adoption decisions [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 14)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Top-management support was decisive**: eight of ten interviewees framed AI adoption as a strategic decision requiring explicit leadership backing, particularly around safety and privacy [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 12)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md). One interviewee for the SISAM case went further, saying they would hesitate to even start an AI project without explicit top-management support, cited as evidence that AI development in the public sector carries considerable risk given the maturity of the technology [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 16)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **No formal business case was needed for SISAM.** Contrary to prior literature (Chui et al., 2018) holding that a solid AI business case is a prerequisite for successful transformation, SISAM's development was pushed by senior leadership and executed internally without one — suggesting public-sector AI adoption can diverge from private-sector patterns [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 16)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Human accountability was treated as non-negotiable**: one director interviewed stated that a machine that cannot be accountable for an outcome cannot be allowed to make the decision; the paper concludes all decisions remain human decisions, with AI recommendation systems in an accessory role, and SISAM reports its reasoning in natural language while field agents retain discretion to independently examine any declaration [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 13)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Compatibility/integration with existing ICT tools** was cited by five interviewees as an adoption facilitator, and partnerships were valued for pooling technical knowledge and financial/human resources while reducing risk [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 12)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Measured outcomes**: SISAM adoption cut the number of goods physically checked during customs clearance by roughly 25% while improving compliance with tax administrative requirements, plus indirect benefits — cross-agent consistency in declaration screening, importers becoming more diligent to avoid flagged deviations, and an increase in public perception of agent impartiality and professionalism [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 16)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Twelve recommendations** for public-sector AI adoption were derived from the literature review, the driver/code analysis, and the SISAM case jointly — spanning legal/regulatory analysis, transparent accountability structures, organizational-readiness assessment, bias safeguards, data strategy, public-private partnership networks, internal technical-expertise plans, and public AI-procurement design [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 17)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).
- **Environmental cost flagged**: the paper cites an EPA estimate that training a large ML model can carry a carbon footprint equivalent to driving a passenger vehicle over 242,000 miles, and argues for sustainability-oriented metrics, energy-efficient hardware, and offset investment as part of responsible AI adoption [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 18)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md).

## Notable quotes

- *"Effective management involvement ensures that our projects align with organizational goals and adhere to ethical and regulatory standards, fostering a sense of trust in our AI initiatives."* — unnamed public official interviewee [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 12)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md)
- *"If a machine cannot be accountable for the outcome, it cannot make the decision."* — Director, large indirect public administration company, São Paulo [(guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector p. 13)](../sources/guedes-oliveira-2024-ai-public-sector.md)

## Use as citation

An **external comparative reference**, useful for:
- Benchmarking the Zurich sandbox's own findings on leadership support, transparency, and human-accountability-by-design against a non-European, tax/customs-domain case (SISAM).
- A second, independently-derived ranking of AI-adoption drivers to compare against the sandbox booklet's Technology/Legal/Organisation framing.
- Its literature-review observation that only ~7% of public-administration AI literature (2013–2023) explicitly addresses *adoption* rather than application or ethics — a framing gap the Zurich corpus itself exists partly to fill.

**Do not** cite this source as if it were a Zurich sandbox pilot, and do not attach it to any `phase`, `sector`, or `partner_role` value from the Zurich taxonomy — those enums describe the Zurich portfolio specifically.
