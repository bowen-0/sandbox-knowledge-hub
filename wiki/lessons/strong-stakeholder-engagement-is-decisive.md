# Strong stakeholder engagement is decisive

The Build & Share booklet states this as its first organisational finding, under the heading «Starke Einbindung entscheidet über Erfolg» (strong involvement decides success). From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] §04 Organisation:

> *«Erfolgreiche Projekte zeichnen sich durch engagierte Umsetzungspartner aus, die Zeit, Ressourcen, Netzwerk und fachliches Feedback einbringen (z. B. umfangreiche Tests im Smart-Parking-Projekt in Frauenfeld). Die Erwartungen an den Umsetzungspartner müssen von Anfang an klar formuliert sein.»* [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 19)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

In English: successful projects are characterised by committed implementation partners who contribute time, resources, network, and expert feedback (for example the extensive testing in the Smart Parking project in Frauenfeld); expectations towards implementation partners must be clearly formulated from the outset.

## The pattern across the corpus

Compare:

| Project | Partner engagement intensity | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| [[smart-parking]] (Frauenfeld) | Extensive — city resources, prolonged testing, post-pilot scaling | Production deployment, multi-canton roll-out |
| [[building-permits]] (Stadt Kloten) | Extensive — real applications, expert validation, multi-vendor coordination | Working prototype + 3,336-eval benchmark, postulate input |
| [[bridge-monitoring]] (SOB pivot) | Strong — physical access, train-traffic data, structural-engineering expertise | Successful real-world testing despite original-partner withdrawal |
| [[medical-documentation]] | Partner-data access blocked by regulation | Analysis-only outcome (still valuable) |
| [[inspection-robots]] | No suitable test partner identified | Analysis-only outcome |

The two analysis-only projects pivoted *not* because the model was wrong, but because partner engagement could not reach the required intensity [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 9)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## How to apply

> [!ai-deployment]
> Securing committed partners for your own AI pilot:
>
> 1. **Pre-define partner expectations in writing**: time commitment, resource contribution, decision-making authority, data-sharing scope.
> 2. **Identify partner-side champions**: individual contributors who personally own the engagement and have authority to unblock decisions.
> 3. **Plan engagement intensity at the budget level**: a partner relationship requiring ~10 hours/week of project-side investment is a different project shape than one requiring ~2.

> [!sandbox-operations]
> Selecting and managing partner commitment across a programme portfolio:
>
> 1. **Choose partners who can commit, not just who are interested.** Municipalities with operational autonomy tend to engage more deeply than larger institutions with diffuse approval processes.
> 2. **Formulate expectations towards implementation partners at intake**, before a project enters the portfolio; the booklet states this must happen from the outset [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 19)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
> 3. **Treat engagement intensity as an early-warning signal across the portfolio**: both Phase II analysis-only outcomes trace back to partner engagement that could not reach the required intensity [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 9)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
