# Continuous regulatory learning beats rigid approval mechanisms

The Phase II booklet [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]] states the principle in its §04 legal insights [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 18)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md):

> *"Continuous regulatory learning becomes critical: traditional regulatory approaches reach their limits with dynamic, adaptive systems. New formats for exchange and feedback between authorities and providers are gaining importance beyond rigid approval mechanisms."*

## Evidence base

Classical product regulation assumes a *finite snapshot*: a product is approved at version 1.0; deviations require new approval. AI systems break this assumption — models are continuously retrained, prompts change, integrations evolve. A regulator who can only react to *applications for approval* loses oversight.

The Phase II corpus surfaces three concrete formats that replace or complement rigid approval:

1. **Regulatory roundtables** ([[regulatory-roundtable]], [[medical-documentation]]) — supervisor + multiple providers in continuous dialogue.
2. **Cross-border sandbox collaboration** ([[inspection-robots]]) — supervisors from different jurisdictions co-learning.
3. **AI-management-system standards** ([[iso-iec-42001]]) — process-level requirements (governance, monitoring) instead of artifact-level approval.

## How to apply

If you're a regulator: build feedback mechanisms with providers; don't wait for approval applications. If you're a provider: engage supervisory authorities continuously; don't wait until certification.
