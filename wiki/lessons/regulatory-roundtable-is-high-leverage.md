# Regulatory roundtables are a high-leverage knowledge-transfer format

[[medical-documentation]] established a transfer format that scales the value of a single regulatory engagement to 10+ AI providers simultaneously. The booklet flags this as a model worth replicating across sectors, listing regulatory roundtables among its standing transfer formats [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 21)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

The booklet's §03 project summary states the finding directly: *"This format proved particularly effective, as AI providers rarely have direct access to supervisory authorities."* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 10)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

## What it is

A scheduled multi-party meeting [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 10)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md) with:

- **AI providers** in a related problem space (10+ start-ups in the case of [[medical-documentation]]).
- **Supervisory authorities** with regulatory remit ([[swissmedic]], the data-protection supervisory authority).
- **Domain experts** and **sandbox programme** as convener.

The format focuses on **shared regulatory questions** rather than individual product decisions. The medical-documentation report recommends institutionalising it: standing platforms for regular dialogue between authorities, technology providers, healthcare providers and patient organisations, especially for use cases many actors work on in parallel [(Medical Documentation report, p. 36)](../sources/p2-medical-documentation.md).

## Why it works

The economics are favourable: one supervisor-hour invested by the authority benefits N providers. The supervisor learns about emerging market patterns; providers gain calibrated regulatory expectations. Both sides reduce coordination cost.

> [!ai-deployment]
> For a team deploying or selling an AI system, the same finding reads as an instruction: direct access to supervisory authorities is rare, so join a regulatory roundtable if a sandbox or sector programme convenes one in your problem space, and if none exists, ask the supervisor or a convening programme for one. Participants gain calibrated regulatory expectations at low coordination cost.

## How to apply

When scaling regulatory clarity in an AI vertical:

1. **Identify a regulator** with substantive remit over the use case.
2. **Aggregate providers** in the same problem space (often there are 5-20 globally; fewer in Switzerland).
3. **Pre-circulate a question agenda** — the roundtable should not re-discover what bilateral conversations could have established.
4. **Document outcomes** in a shareable form (e.g. a guideline; see [[medical-documentation]]'s practice-oriented guideline, *praxisnaher Leitfaden* [(Build & Share (Phase II overview report), p. 10)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)).
