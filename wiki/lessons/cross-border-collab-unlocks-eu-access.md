# Cross-border sandbox collaboration unlocks EU market access

Switzerland is not in the EU, but Swiss AI vendors often need EU market access. The Phase II [[inspection-robots]] project established the canonical pattern: a Swiss-led sandbox engaging supervisory authorities in **Germany** ([[bundesnetzagentur-de]]) and **Liechtenstein** ([[li-digital-innovation]]) to clarify EU AI Act + Machinery Regulation classification for a Swiss-built product.

From [[00-overview-phase2-build-and-share]], section 03 («Autonome Inspektionsroboter», p. 14):

> *«Erstmals arbeitete das Sandbox-Projekt zudem grenzüberschreitend mit zuständigen Aufsichtsbehörden zusammen – der Bundesnetzagentur in Deutschland und der Stabsstelle für Digitale Innovation des Fürstentums Liechtenstein.»* [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 14)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md)

In translation: for the first time, the sandbox project also collaborated across borders with the competent supervisory authorities, namely the Federal Network Agency in Germany and the Office for Digital Innovation of the Principality of Liechtenstein.

## Evidence base

- **[[inspection-robots]]** — the canonical case. The booklet states the project's goal as systematically clarifying the regulatory prerequisites for EU market access, along a concrete [[anybotics]] use case under the [[eu-ai-act]] and the [[machinery-regulation]] [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 14)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
- The same page credits the outcome explicitly: *«Damit leistete die Sandbox einen konkreten Beitrag zum EU-Marktzugang für Schweizer Robotikunternehmen.»* In translation: the sandbox thereby made a concrete contribution to EU market access for Swiss robotics companies [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 14)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
- This is *not* a regulatory shortcut — the EU regulations still apply in full. What changes is the **coordination cost**: a Swiss vendor would otherwise need to navigate each EU member-state authority bilaterally.

> [!sandbox-operations]
> For programme operators, the transferable move is the convening itself. The sandbox provides a structured convening surface: by bringing the competent foreign authorities ([[bundesnetzagentur-de]], [[li-digital-innovation]]) into a domestic project, it spared the vendor bilateral navigation of each authority. This was the programme's first cross-border engagement with non-Swiss regulators [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 14)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).

## How to apply

> [!ai-deployment]
> If you're a Swiss AI vendor planning to enter the EU market:
>
> 1. **Pre-classify your product** under the [[eu-ai-act]] risk taxonomy (prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk, minimal-risk) and any sectoral regulation that applies (e.g. [[machinery-regulation]] for autonomous systems).
> 2. **Identify which EU supervisory authorities** are relevant for your product class and target markets.
> 3. **Engage early** — via the sandbox programme or directly — rather than at certification time. See [[integrate-regulation-early]].
> 4. **Consider piloting [[iso-iec-42001]]** as a structured operationalisation of AI-management requirements, as [[inspection-robots]] did with [[modulos]] [(00-overview-phase2-build-and-share p. 14)](../sources/00-overview-phase2-build-and-share.md).
